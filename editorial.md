# Editorial Guidelines

## Purpose

This document defines the editorial standards for the Semantic
Archaeology lexicon.

## Core Principle

Replace a common English word with the literal meaning of its
etymological roots---not its modern definition.

## Inclusion Criteria

-   Common contemporary English word.
-   Well-attested etymology.
-   Literal gloss is vivid, concrete, and concise.
-   Gloss functions naturally in running prose.
-   Surprise outweighs explanation.

## Exclude

-   Transparent compounds (churchyard, handbook, weekday).
-   Folk etymologies.
-   Acronym stories.
-   Speculative derivations without scholarly support.
-   Entries that produce little estrangement.

## Recovery Types

-   `literal` --- literal recovery of the root image.
-   `conceptual` --- recovers an historical practice or material image.
-   `semantic` --- recovers an earlier historical sense (for a future
    companion lexicon).
-   `contested` --- scholarship has no consensus; present uncertainty
    rather than inventing a gloss.

## Repository

-   canonical.json --- accepted entries.
-   candidates.json --- research queue.
-   editorial.md --- editorial policy.
-   poetics.md --- conceptual essay.

## Metadata

Each entry should eventually include: - part_of_speech - image_family -
surprise_score - frequency_score - confidence_score - verified -
verification_source - editorial_notes - recovery_type

## Editorial Goal

Curate a lexicon of forgotten images rather than an exhaustive
etymological dictionary. Prefer density, surprise, and philological
integrity over coverage.
